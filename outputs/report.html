Voice-of-customer study · Google Play, India · 2026-07-27 to 2026-09-25

What India's quick-commerce customers complain about, and what each app should fix first

128,229 public reviews of Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart and bigbasket. 37,812 had enough text to classify; Claude labelled 2,000 of them and a distilled classifier labelled the rest.

Key findings
  • Zepto has a recovery problem, not a speed problem. It rates 3.29★ against 4.06–4.15★ for its peers, and its gap is concentrated in what happens after an order goes wrong. Complaint rates vs the peer average: customer support (3.9×), product quality (4.0×), missing / wrong items (4.2×), refunds & returns (3.9×).
  • The same after-sales loop drives churn. The complaints most often paired with "I'm leaving" are customer support, missing / wrong items, refunds & returns (up to 9% of reviews raising them).
  • Customer support is the most common complaint across the category, and delivery speed costs the most rating per mention (1.30★).
  • Replying is not resolving. Every app answers 93%+ of 1–2★ reviews within a median 12 minutes, which points to templated replies. Resolution quality, not reply rate, is the metric to manage.

Method and limitations

  • Reviews collected from Google Play (India) with reviewer names and photos removed at collection.
  • Claude (Anthropic) labelled a stratified sample against a 12-topic taxonomy built around the customer journey; a TF-IDF + logistic-regression classifier trained on those labels scaled them to all reviews.
  • The distilled classifier reproduces Claude's complaint labels with a macro F1 of 0.69 (5-fold out-of-fold), at near-zero cost per review.
  • App-store reviewers skew towards extreme experiences, so rates describe reviewers, not all customers.
  • Rating impact comes from a regression with app fixed effects. It shows association, not causation.
